# Importance of Cryptography

## What is Cryptography?
Cryptography is the practice and study of techniques used to secure communication and protect data, even when attackers or unauthorized people are present.

Its main goal is to ensure that:
- Unauthorized people **cannot read** the data.
- Unauthorized people **cannot modify** the data.

In simple words, cryptography keeps information **private, accurate, and trustworthy**.

---

# Main Security Goals of Cryptography

## 1. Confidentiality
Ensures that only authorized people can read the data.

**Example:**
- Your username and password are encrypted when you log into TryHackMe or any website.

---

## 2. Integrity
Ensures that data has not been changed or tampered with during storage or transmission.

**Example:**
- A downloaded file can be verified using a **hash function** to make sure it hasn't been modified.

---

## 3. Authenticity
Verifies that the sender or website is genuine.

**Example:**
- During online banking, your browser checks the server's **digital certificate** to confirm you are communicating with your real bank and not an attacker.

---

# Real-World Uses of Cryptography

## Secure Website Login
When you log into websites like TryHackMe:
- Your login credentials are encrypted.
- Attackers cannot steal them by intercepting network traffic.

---

## SSH Connections
When connecting to a remote machine using SSH:
- SSH creates an encrypted tunnel.
- No one can eavesdrop on your session.

---

## Online Banking
Your browser:
- Checks the website's digital certificate.
- Confirms the server belongs to your bank.
- Establishes an encrypted connection.

---

## File Verification
After downloading a file:
- Hash functions verify that the downloaded file is exactly the same as the original.
- This confirms the file wasn't corrupted or modified.

---

# Why Cryptography is Important

Cryptography protects sensitive information both:

- **At Rest** → Data stored on devices or servers.
- **In Motion** → Data being transmitted over a network.

Without cryptography:
- Passwords could be stolen.
- Bank transactions could be intercepted.
- Medical records could be exposed.
- Files could be modified without anyone noticing.

---

# Industry Standards and Regulations

Many industries legally require the use of cryptography.

## PCI DSS (Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard)

Used by organizations that handle credit card information.

Requirements include:
- Encrypt stored card data (**at rest**).
- Encrypt transmitted card data (**in motion**).

---

## Healthcare Data Regulations

Medical records must also be protected using cryptography.

Examples include:

- **HIPAA** – United States
- **HITECH** – United States
- **GDPR** – European Union
- **DPA (Data Protection Act)** – United Kingdom

These laws require organizations to protect sensitive patient information.

# Key Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Confidentiality | Prevents unauthorized access to data. |
| Integrity | Ensures data has not been altered. |
| Authenticity | Confirms the identity of the sender or server. |
| Encryption | Converts readable data into unreadable form to protect it. |
| Hash Function | Verifies that data has not been changed. |
| Digital Certificate | Confirms the identity of a website or server. |
| Data at Rest | Data stored on a device or server. |
| Data in Motion | Data being transmitted over a network. |

---

# Summary

- Cryptography secures communication and protects data from attackers.
- It provides **Confidentiality, Integrity, and Authenticity (CIA)**.
- It is used daily in:
  - Website logins
  - SSH connections
  - Online banking
  - File verification
- It protects data while it is:
  - Stored (**at rest**)
  - Transmitted (**in motion**)
- Many industries, including banking and healthcare, legally require cryptography through standards like **PCI DSS**, **HIPAA**, **HITECH**, **GDPR**, and **DPA**.
