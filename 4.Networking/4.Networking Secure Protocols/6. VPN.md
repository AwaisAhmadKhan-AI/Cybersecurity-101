# VPN (Virtual Private Network)

A **VPN (Virtual Private Network)** creates a **secure, encrypted connection** between a device (or an entire network) and a remote network over the Internet.

It allows users to securely access private resources as if they were directly connected to the organization's local network.

---

# Why Do We Need a VPN?

The Internet was designed to deliver data efficiently, **not to protect its confidentiality**.

Without additional security:

- Data can be intercepted.
- Sensitive information may be exposed.
- Attackers may attempt to modify transmitted data.

A VPN solves this problem by **encrypting network traffic**, ensuring secure communication over public networks.

---

# How a VPN Works

1. A VPN client connects to a VPN server.
2. The client and server establish an **encrypted tunnel**.
3. All data sent through the tunnel is encrypted.
4. The VPN server decrypts the data and forwards it to its destination.
5. Responses are encrypted again before being sent back to the client.

As a result, anyone monitoring the network only sees encrypted traffic.

---

# VPN Tunnel

A **VPN tunnel** is the encrypted connection between the VPN client and the VPN server.

Inside this tunnel:

- Data is encrypted.
- Traffic is protected from eavesdropping.
- Communication remains private over the public Internet.

---

# Common Uses of VPNs

## 1. Secure Remote Access

Employees can securely connect to their company's internal network from anywhere.

Example:
- Working from home
- Traveling
- Accessing office resources remotely

---

## 2. Connecting Multiple Offices

Organizations with offices in different locations can connect all branches through VPNs.

This allows users at different sites to access shared resources as if they were on the same local network.

---

## 3. Privacy on Public Networks

When connected to a VPN:

- Your Internet traffic is encrypted.
- Your Internet Service Provider (ISP) cannot easily inspect your browsing activity.
- Public Wi-Fi becomes much safer to use.

---

## 4. Changing Your Public IP Address

When connected to a VPN, websites usually see **the VPN server's public IP address instead of yours**.

For example:

- Connect to a VPN server in Japan.
- Websites may believe you are browsing from Japan.
- Some websites will automatically display Japanese content or services available in that region.

---

# Benefits of Using a VPN

- Encrypts Internet traffic.
- Protects sensitive information.
- Enables secure remote access.
- Connects multiple office locations securely.
- Hides your public IP address from websites.
- Helps protect privacy on public Wi-Fi networks.

---

# Limitations of VPNs

A VPN does **not** guarantee complete anonymity.

Some VPN services may:

- Leak your real IP address.
- Leak DNS requests (DNS Leak).
- Route only part of your traffic through the VPN (Split Tunneling).

Because of this, additional privacy tests (such as DNS leak tests) may be necessary.

---

# Important Notes

- Most VPNs route **all Internet traffic** through the encrypted tunnel.
- Some VPN configurations only provide access to a private network and **do not** route all Internet traffic.
- The effectiveness of a VPN depends on its configuration and provider.

---

# VPN Components

| Component | Purpose |
|-----------|---------|
| VPN Client | Creates the secure connection from the user's device. |
| VPN Server | Receives, decrypts, and forwards VPN traffic. |
| VPN Tunnel | The encrypted connection between client and server. |
